01 / The replication thesis

Replicate the operating model, not the species list

Every district needs the same decision discipline, but may need different crops, water systems, livelihood safeguards and land-management rules.

1. Protect life and learning

Link cash or food support, nutrition screening, school meals and safe water without waiting for regeneration outcomes.

Potential benefit: days–weeks if delivery systems already function. Conditional, not a service promise.

2. Repair what already exists

Audit well and pump uptime, drinking-water safety, storage, local spare parts, school kitchens and supplier payments.

Potential benefit: days–months subject to testing, parts, finance and competent operators.

3. Regenerate what belongs there

Conserve native vegetation, restore soil function and protect watersheds with validated tenure and biodiversity safeguards.

Potential benefit: seasons–years; no tree-planting quotas as ecological success proxies.

Most actionable policy bridge: Connect Ministry of Education home-grown school feeding, the PITAF learning program, BNGRC anticipatory action, local water-operator maintenance and commune-level land governance in one auditable district agreement. Existing programs are real; this proposed agreement is not an announced government program. [4] [5] [6]
02 / Biophysical differentiation

Choose by ecological fit, then by local readiness

Geographical categories overlap. Elevation bands are descriptive, not GIS polygon classifications. Verify each site with an authoritative map, soil survey, water-quality testing and community knowledge before intervention.

04 / Place-based pilots

Candidate district pathways, not site selection

These are proposed investigation areas with different needs, not endorsed project sites. A pilot cannot be considered selected until local authorities and community groups validate access, need, rights, operators and funding.

06 / Field assessment

Is this community ready to pilot?

Seven prerequisites help avoid the false precision of a numeric readiness score. “Unknown” blocks a scale decision. This tool stores only answers in your browser when you save.

Local evidence checklist

Gate assessment

Complete the checks to view an evidence-based stop/go summary.

What counts as credible evidence?

Minutes from a genuine local consultation; water test reports; signed maintenance responsibility; explicit procurement budget; mapped rights; dated forecast and receipt chain; local-language education review.

This is procedural screening, not a guarantee of safety, climate impact, eligibility or funding.

07 / Implementation sequence

Execute a small, measurable bridge before copying it

Milestones represent demonstrated work rather than gamified claims of lives saved. Mark complete only when verifiable artifacts exist.

0 of 8 evidence milestones complete

Illustrative time sequence

Days 0–14
Publish local baseline and rights/consent map; inventory water points, schools and aid pathways; connect existing responders.
Weeks 3–12
Repair validated assets, protect school meals and create rainfall/price-trigger playbooks; secure spare parts and response budgets.
First growing season
Test a few farmer-selected crops, soil and seed practices alongside protected ecological comparison plots; record failures.
Months 6–24
Replicate only where food safety, water reliability, ecological outcomes, governance and equity measures meet local criteria.

Replication stop rules

  • Unsafe drinking water, unverified chemistry or no testing capacity.
  • Livelihood restoration would displace people or violate land rights.
  • Invasive/non-native species or native habitat conversion proposed without review.
  • No solvent operator for repairs, purchases or maintenance.
  • Women, children, migrants or pastoral users are excluded from decisions.
  • No way to record harms, correct errors or stop an activity.

A stop is a governance success, not a program failure.

Evidence standard and open questions

Facts: statements explicitly documented by cited sources. Analytical inferences: proposed linkages or repurposings of those programs. Pilot hypotheses: plausible interventions whose performance in a location is unknown. Illustrative timing: first-plausible-benefit windows, not guaranteed schedules.

Missing before implementation: validated commune boundaries and land-cover overlays, local elevation and slope grids, hydrological feasibility, ecological surveys and species provenance, signed local governance agreements, school enrollment and supplier access, fund flow, market price series, maintenance baseline, mobility/disability assessment, and feedback channels.

For any national scale-up use primary administrative units, documented denominators, and a regularly updated data steward; no unverified geospatial percentages or affected-population allocations appear in this release.

Zero-harm / anti-inversion: Food, safe water and education are rights, not rewards. No coercive labor, conditional food-for-tree-planting, biometric enrollment, covert surveillance, habitat conversion or involuntary relocation. Transparent grievances and human oversight are mandatory.
