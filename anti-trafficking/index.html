A decision-support framework for Madagascar's PGE-R anti-trafficking implementation, safe labour mobility, community-managed water, school nutrition and regenerative enterprise. Built for Malagasy policymakers, operating partners and prospective funders.

🛡️ Survivor protection+🌱 Regenerative resilience+📊 Accountable finance

Independent public-interest prototype by Foster + Navi / Planetary Restoration Archive. Not an official Malagasy government or funder platform.

Government direction • 19 Oct 2026

BNLTEH + PGE-R

Bring anti-trafficking implementation under national planning; prioritize protection of jobseekers abroad and safe labour mobility. [S01]

Reported administrative baseline • 2026
171 identified · 33 referred

U.S. TIP report figures, not a national prevalence estimate. Referral capacity warrants scrutiny. [S02]

Illustrative public programme • first 12 months
MGA 8–15 bn

Research-derived planning band, not an adopted budget or new award. Requires bottom-up costing.

Financing principle

Separate ledgers

Protection and core public services use grants/budgets. Commercial returns, if any, come only from legitimate, voluntary regenerative businesses.

Decision boundary: This is an actionable proposal, not a claim that the named funders have pledged to this toolkit. Current project allocations are not transferable without formal consent, eligibility review and procurement rules. Investor outputs are simulations, not offers or guaranteed returns.

The integrated causal pathway

Climate & income shocks→Household vulnerability→Safer jobs + meals + water→Lower exposure to exploitative recruitment

This is a theory of change, not an established causal effect. Trafficking risk must be independently measured; a decline in identified cases does not by itself establish success.

01 / National operating mandate

From executive direction to service delivery

Translate the October 19 Primature announcement into a costed, accountable PGE-R workstream. [S01]

Prime Minister's Office

Strategic oversight, cross-ministerial dispute resolution and budget accountability; do not create a duplicate apex body.

BNLTEH

Proposed permanent programme secretariat: minimum referral standards, regional coordination, data quality, quarterly reporting.

Labour + Foreign Affairs

Recruiter registration, job-offer verification, worker contracts, consular protection and bilateral corridor governance, starting with Mauritius as a measurable pilot.

Health + Social + Justice

Presumed-victim assistance independent of criminal conviction; child-sensitive pathways, professional casework, investigations and wage recovery.

Operational chain: no unfunded handoffs

First contact→Reasonable-grounds screening→Consent + immediate safety→Service handoff & funded case plan→Optional longitudinal follow-up
Mandate and financial control checkpoints
  1. Map legally assigned ministry competencies and existing action plans before changing mandates.
  2. Assign one owner, one receiving service, one funding source, one evidence standard to each programme action.
  3. Document whether the Mauritius labour arrangement authorizes each proposed safeguard; the full agreement was not independently analysed.
  4. Contract worker and survivor organizations as advisers/service providers with safeguarding, payment and conflict-of-interest protocols.
  5. Publish budget execution and aggregated service outcomes without personal case records.
02 / Investable only when appropriate

Six complementary implementation packages

Packages are modular. Co-locate services at district level where residents and local institutions agree; do not make survivor benefits conditional on other programme enrolment.

P1 · Presumed-victim referral, protection and reintegration PUBLIC / GRANT ONLY

Prototype: 24-month service-system pilot, illustrative envelope US$1.2 million. Finance safe accommodation, accessible casework, emergency transport, child protection, legal assistance, independent complaints and 6/12/24-month voluntary aftercare.

Leads to consult: BNLTEH, legally responsible social/health agencies, UNICEF, IOM, qualified civil society and worker groups. Returns: survivor safety, service access, time to protection—never investor cash yield. [S03] [S04]

P2 · Fair recruitment + Mauritius corridor pilot PUBLIC / GRANT ONLY

Prototype: 24 months, US$750,000 for recruiter census, public licence register, independent job-offer validation, worker-language contracts, fee audit, complaint response and consular referral procedures. No worker tracking or travel bans.

Outcome indicators: proportion of audited offers, worker-paid fees, contract substitution, resolved complaints, stranded-worker response time. Draw on SENAMAMA and ILO fair recruitment experience. [S05]

P3 · School meals + community water + household shock response GRANT / PUBLIC PROCUREMENT

Prototype: 36 months, US$2.4 million across selected consenting communes; forecast-triggered household support, maintenance-funded drinking water, home-grown school meals, local seed availability and independent nutrition monitoring.

Approach: align with WFP school feeding, World Bank safety nets/water systems, local school authorities and village water committees rather than replace them. Nutrition and safe water remain entitlements, not profit-bearing concessions. [S06] [S07]

P4 · Solar-assisted cooperative irrigation and crop services BLENDED, RING-FENCED

Prototype: US$300,000 repayable commercial capital plus independently budgeted US$250,000 grant/public component for watershed/ecological work and inclusion. Commercial revenue: voluntary equipment services, agricultural service contracts and crop aggregation under fair contracts.

Controls: water rights review, hydrologic carrying capacity, land consent, affordable service commitments, maintenance reserves, fair wages, cooperative governance and environmental assessment. The commercial capital model below is explicitly hypothetical. [S08]

P5 · Climate-resilient crop aggregation, storage and fair offtake BLENDED, RING-FENCED

Prototype: US$500,000 commercial capital and separate technical-assistance grants (illustrative US$350,000). Potential cash revenues: transparent storage, quality services and produce sales with enforceable producer prices.

Review compatibility with GIZ/PrAda 2+ and IFAD/DEFIS+ instead of presuming funds are accessible. No land grabbing, exclusive exploitative offtake or unverified carbon-credit projections. [S08] [S09]

P6 · Local regenerative enterprise + worker-owned value addition BLENDED, RING-FENCED

Prototype: US$400,000 commercial capital for productive solar and value-add equipment; separately seek public/grant funding for training, disability access and capacity building. Cash revenue: documented service offtake from participating businesses.

Safeguards: worker ownership options, wage/health protections, child-labour due diligence, contracts and exit rights; community-led site selection. Links to AfDB jobs/institutional reforms and UNIDO/GEF green-enterprise work are alignment opportunities, not confirmed partnerships. [S10] [S11]

05 / Interactive cash flow simulator

Finance the public good. Model enterprise returns separately.

A configurable six- to ten-year cash-flow model for commercial services only. The model does not monetize survivor data, referrals, statutory services, ecosystem credits without signed purchase agreements, or exploitation avoidance as investor receipts.

Ledger A · protection and essential services

0% investor yield

Appropriated budgets, accountable grants and public procurement finance P1–P3. Evaluate timeliness, wellbeing, nutrition, water reliability, inclusion, wage recovery and local ownership. No interest, dividends or return rights arise from survivors' outcomes.

Indicative national anti-trafficking new programme spend: MGA 8–15bn in year one, 15–30bn/year at scale-up, 20–40bn/year at maturity. The historical-research figures are unadopted planning bands.

Ledger B · community-aligned enterprises

Investor repayment may come from lawful sales, voluntary farmer service purchases, contracted aggregation, repair and productive-energy services. The local institution must retain informed negotiating rights and water/land protections.

These are illustrative parameters only. No audited site business case, market demand, tax opinion, pricing study, secured contracts or independently verified investment return has been established.

Enter USD to avoid fabricated exchange rates. Reserves are modeled as growing with operating inflation. Separate grant amount is disclosed for context and is excluded from investor cashflows/returns. Investor capital is entirely at risk.

07 / Milestones and decision authority

Phased implementation and scale gates

0–90 days · establish baseline

Map and cost, protect immediately

BNLTEH-led mapping of referral capacity, recruiters, consular corridors, budget lines, drought-affected service gaps and local partner institutions. Fund urgent protection from day one; do not defer assistance pending studies.

Gate: signed minimum referral SOP, validated local provider directory and costed results framework.

3–12 months · district pilots

Operationalize and audit

Safe job-offer checks; regional multidisciplinary teams; school and water site feasibility; community feedback procedures; independent labour and environmental safeguards.

Gate: median referral time, reliable budgets, independent community consultation and baseline worker/household measures.

12–36 months · adapt and expand

Scale what survives scrutiny

Expand multidisciplinary access, evaluate Mauritius corridor, deploy rigorous enterprise pilots and add long-term follow-up. Preserve regional adaptation rather than copying a nationally uniform design.

Gate: independently audited costs, risk-adjusted enterprise projections and demonstrable beneficiary/worker outcomes.

3–5+ years · permanent capability

Recurrent funding, independent evaluation

Regular domestic appropriations for essential services; regional ownership of infrastructure; repeating labour-risk surveys and publicly auditable aggregate programme budgets.

Gate: sustained service coverage and ecological/worker outcomes without permanent emergency grants.

09 / Zero harm and anti-inversion

Rights, consent, and safeguards before leverage

Non-coercive protection

No forced repatriation, detention, disclosure or police cooperation as a condition of support; specific duties under applicable law require local legal review. Children's best interests prevail.

No exploitative finance

Never collateralize workers, children, referrals, welfare entitlements, survivor data or access to essential water. Investors cannot receive trafficking “success bonuses.”

Community ownership

Demonstrate meaningful consent, accessible participation, locally negotiated benefit sharing, grievance independence and transparent distribution of risks and profits.

Ecological prudence

Protect water tables, existing ecosystems, biodiversity, genetic diversity and tenure rights. Prefer locally appropriate/native vegetation; assess proposed introductions for invasiveness and disease risk.

Data sovereignty

No surveillance, ad tracking, analytics, hidden telemetry, beneficiary databases or network requests from this file. Investor-facing metrics must be aggregated, not personal.

Integrity and procurement

Conflict-of-interest declarations, beneficial ownership screening, independent accounting, independent audits, competitive procurement and accessible appeal procedures.

Stop-work / remedy triggers
  • Credible exploitation allegations implicating staff or contractors: protect affected people and initiate impartial response without punishing complainants.
  • Water withdrawals exceed safe ecological limits or harm neighbouring customary users: pause service expansion and review.
  • Local community rejects land or operational terms: do not proceed or characterize silence as consent.
  • Unverified claims of economic return, carbon credit or grant eligibility: remove them from partner solicitations.
  • Privacy breach or unsafe public dashboard disclosure: stop publication, remediate and notify where legally required.
