02 / HOW THE PIECES CONNECT

From observation to locally owned delivery.

This is a proposed sequence, not a claim that any intervention has been commissioned, tested, or completed in the field.

01 · LISTEN

Local authority first

Identify customary and formal stewards, rights holders, local practitioners and existing organizations. No presumed consent.

02 · BASELINE

Map what exists

Compare ecological suitability, households' priorities, land tenure, nutrition and reliable water before choosing a measure.

03 · PROTECT

Set stop conditions

Child safeguarding, labour rights, biodiversity, procurement integrity, do-no-harm review and accessible grievance routes.

04 · CO-DESIGN

Budget with communities

Build a small service plan with local operations and maintenance, procurement assumptions and transparent ownership.

05 · VERIFY

Pilot and measure

Track independently observable outputs and outcomes. Include costs, adverse events, nonparticipation and failures.

06 · ADAPT

Replicate mechanisms

Share the operating model only after it fits a second community's ecology, governance and legal context.

Regenerative safety rule:

Never substitute planting counts for restored ecosystem function, funding pledges for delivered services, or citations for informed local consent. Protect native grasslands and wetlands; do not infer that a species is safe to introduce because it grows elsewhere.

Zero-harm / anti-inversion checks

  • Community veto and free, prior informed consent where applicable.
  • Do not disclose survivors', minors' or complainants' identities.
  • Do not plant non-native species in natural habitats without lawful ecological review.
  • Separate forecast estimates, scenarios and approved funding from actual delivery.
  • Independent grievance channels and verification before scale-up.
  • Local ownership, transparent procurement and reversible pilots.

The archive is a research and planning resource, not legal, medical, agronomic or financial professional authorization.
